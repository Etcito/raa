  (the installed Direct + Ambient slots, evaluated in material_main) is carried in emissive,
  which the shell pass does not darken, and base_color keeps 40 %; the root darkening range
  shrinks from 0.35..1 to 0.74..1, the overall brightness stays, and the sheen is counted
  1.6x (more velvet toward the edges). Side effect: the instance tint (applied to
  base_color after material_main) reaches only the 40 % share on raa.plush.
- `examples/lookdev/scenes/m10-materials.js`: the plush sets stand on the inflatables'
  glossy clear-coated floor (`PLUSH_FLOOR`, roughness 0.12, coat 1 / 0.05, same blue).

## Tuning plush against the judge (card 4750), 2026-10-24

Starting point: the first m10 capture (card 4747) ran without `--judge`, so the `plush`,
`fur-on` and `fur-off` records carry no judge score, and their PNGs did not reach this card
(the three captureSha256 differ, so the fur pair is not a stand-in). Every value below was
chosen on SwiftShader previews of the lookdev page at 1280x720 (sandbox Chrome with
`--enable-unsafe-webgpu --enable-features=Vulkan --use-vulkan=swiftshader
--use-angle=swiftshader` over a CDP pipe, 4 frames 400 ms apart, ~110 s per frame with fur),
placed beside crops of the ref-1 and ref-2 penguins. Previews only, never records.

Diagnosis: the strands were far below a pixel. The shell pass sizes a strand cell as
1 / sqrt(density): 1200 strands per cm2 is a 0.29 mm cell, while a pixel is ~3.4 mm at the
2.1 m plush camera (60 deg lens, 720 px). With `fwidth(dist)` of several cells per pixel the
coverage of every shell sat near 0.5, so the fur read as grey salt-and-pepper grain (worst
on the white belly) with an even, offset halo instead of tapering tips.

Values changed and why (`examples/lookdev/scenes/m10-materials.js`, plush sets only):
- `FUR` density 1200 -> 12 per cm2 (cell ~2.9 mm, about a pixel): strands taper through

## Fix: judge inflatables, attempt 2 (card 4786), 2026-10-24

The judge gave 5.0/10: everything present, but the plastic read pale and crude, the left
ring "pink/yellow rather than blue" and the floor flatter than ref-1's.

Diagnosis without a GPU: the failing PNG was inverted through a numpy port of the tonemap
(AgX + punchy) back to HDR. The yellow ball was (0.87, 0.85, 0.54) and the red hammer
panel (0.82, 0.29, 0.46): a large blue term was added to every pixel. Bloom causes it:
the upsample chain adds every mip (raa-bloom's own test: a constant image gains
strength x (levels - 1)), so at 720p (7 levels) strength 0.1 mixed ~0.7x a blurred frame
of bright blue sky and floor into the plastic. Undoing that bloom estimate and re-tonemapping
gave a much more saturated preview (yellow ball still olive under the blue sky fill).

Changes (scene only, `examples/lookdev/scenes/m10-materials.js`, inflatable sets):
- bloom 0.1 -> 0.015; exposure +0.35 -> +0.55 EV (makes up for the energy the haze added);
- sun 7 -> 8 and IBL diffuse 0.7 -> 0.5: less blue cast on the coloured base;
- `YELLOW` [1, 0.84, 0.12] -> [1, 0.74, 0.04] (reads yellow, not olive, under the sky fill);
- floor [0.3, 0.65, 1] roughness 0.18 -> saturated azure [0.12, 0.52, 1] roughness 0.12
  with a clear coat (1, roughness 0.05): sharp sky reflections like ref-1's mats;
- description: the rings are "one blue with yellow panels, one yellow with pink panels".

Friction / what nomorechat should do instead:
- A strength of 0.04..0.1 is not "a little bloom" with this bloom (it scales with the mip
  count); the raa-bloom docs or `setBloom` docs should say so, or the composite should
  normalise by the level count (that would change the neon tuning, so not done here).
- Tuning is still blind: a one-variant preview render for model cards would replace the
  numpy inverse-tonemap estimate used here.
